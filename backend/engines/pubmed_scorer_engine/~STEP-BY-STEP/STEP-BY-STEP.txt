Updated step 1 – per program

Problem

Teams start experiments from “RCT-looking” papers without a strict filter for:

true human relevance,

clinical endpoints,

recency,

and translation risk.

This creates protocol work and spend before the evidence is strong enough.

Solution

A literature-first gating layer that:

auto-screens human RCT literature in a chosen field,

scores evidence quality using metadata proxies,

and enforces a policy:

discard,

cheap-test-only,

or approve and stop.

How it is different from what’s available

Most systems:

summarize,

recommend,

or surface “interesting” papers.

This system:

blocks action unless evidence meets thresholds,

outputs discard decisions as a first-class artifact,

and forces cheap tests instead of big experiments when borderline.

Updated step 2 – per program

Demand

High in:

translational groups,

clinical research offices,

innovation hubs in hospitals,

grant reviewers / internal funding committees.

Because it reduces:

protocol churn,

expensive follow-on studies,

and “false-start” projects.
